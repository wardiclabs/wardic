# A Batch Reorder group: `Reorder.BatchGroup`, a long list reordered as one keyed Batch

A `Reorder.Group` of thousands of like items costs a Wardic element per item (ADR-0020): each item has its own layout projection and drag, and every crossover waits for a Measure pass that measures every item's box. A Batch (ADR-0069, ADR-0070) places every member from its index with nothing to measure, so a reorder can be one Batch change.

#712's prototype (2026-11-11, the Solid 2 binding built for production, headless Chromium 147) measured it: one keyed Batch, one pointer listener on the list, the held item moved through the Batch, the crossover to the nearest place, and the keyboard model of ADR-0037 written again by hand.

| 5,000 items | `Reorder.Group` + `axis` | the Batch, one listener, keyboard |
|---|---|---|
| mount | 1,061 ms | 148 ms |
| a drag: longest frame / main thread per frame | 33 ms / 5.12 ms | 17 ms / 2.91 ms |
| keys: longest frame / main thread per frame | 17 ms / 4.57 ms | 17 ms / 3.03 ms |

At 1,000 items every way held 17 ms frames, and the Batch mounted in 51 to 58 ms against 320 to 440 ms. Keeping the DOM in the list's order cost about 0.45 ms a frame while dragging, outside script. The same prototype found #710.

## Decisions (grilled 2026-11-11, #712)

1. **Its own component pair:** `Reorder.BatchGroup` and `Reorder.BatchItem`, on both bindings (Q7, Q10). An item is typed as a **Batch member**, so a prop only a Wardic element takes (`layoutId`, `variants`, `drag`) is a type error, not a silent no-op. `Reorder.Group` is unchanged.
2. **`BatchGroup` takes `Reorder.Group`'s props and events** on each binding (Q11): `values` and `onReorder`; `onReorderEnd` and the hold callbacks on Solid 1; `keyboard`, `announcements`, `instructions`, `direction` and `as`. On Solid 2, `reorderGroup()` builds its controller over the projection system as it's made, so a `BatchGroup` can't take one: its `groupRef` is a callback handed the group, with `reorderGroup()`'s `next`, `each` and `when` of `reorderEnd`, `holdStart` and `holdEnd`, its `move` and its `axis` (Q25). No primitives like `createReorderGroup` or a `batchReorderGroup()` in the first version.
3. **Places, not boxes** (Q2, Q12, Q16). Every item is one size, and its place comes from its index (*Amended by* *Amendment (#714)*: one size for every item, one for each, or each read from the page):
   - `axis` picks the shape: `"y"`, a column, the default (there's nothing to detect from); `"x"`, a row; `"xy"`, a wrapping grid.
   - `itemSize={{ width, height }}`, `gap` (a number, or `{ x, y }`), and a grid's `columns`, a number, or, left out, as many as the group's width holds, at least one (Q36). Then the group's width is the app's, and it sets only its height; it reads its content width a microtask after it mounts and the frame after each resize, a change of columns placing every item again in one change on the group's transition.
   - `direction="rtl"` mirrors a row's and a grid's places from the group's right edge, and the group's element takes its direction, so the keyboard's arrows, the crossover and the items' static positions all read it. Left out, the group's own `style` direction, else the one its element inherits from its parent, read as it mounts, so a page's `dir="rtl"` needs no prop; a server's is `"ltr"` (Q35). A resize reads a grid's width again, never its direction.
   - **The first read places the items at once**, drawn before the page paints (`Batch.draw`): no flight from a guess, and on hydration nothing moves.
   - The word is *place*: **slot** is a Projection node's box.
   - Items of different sizes are #714 (*Amendment (#714)*).
4. **The group lays its items out** (Q13): it's `position: relative`, sized to its places, so a scroller around it scrolls; each item is `position: absolute` with no `top` or `left`, so it sits at its static position, the start of the group's content box (its padding kept; right to left, at its right edge), sized from `itemSize`, placed by its transform from there (*Amended by* *Amendment (#714)*: sized from its own size, given or read, and laid out in flow until its first size is read). The app styles the rest.
5. **The DOM follows the list's order** (Q3), always: Tab and a screen reader read the DOM. The app renders its items with `<For each={values}>`, as a `Reorder.Group`'s, so Solid moves one node a reorder (Q29); the group moves none itself, as the prototype's `insertBefore` did.
6. **Built on Reorder's layers** (Q5, Q6):
   - **Core's `Reorder.Group` as it is,** fed an `ItemSource` whose box is the item's place. The **Crossover** is Reorder's, Motion's, so both forms feel the same; a reorder waits for the Batch to place the new order, not for a Measure pass.
   - **The DOM controller without a projection system:** items register a plain handle (their source, and how to raise them), the Batch's placing stands in for the Measure pass, and a stepped item is revealed from its place.
   - **The keyboard, its announcements and their overrides, right-to-left, Auto-scroll and `reorderEnd`** are the code `Reorder.Group` runs.
7. **The drag** (Q14, Q17): the group hears a member's press through the delegated feed over its members (ADR-0055 *Amendment (#665)*), then core's drag recognizer runs for that one member: the threshold, samples and 100 ms velocity of a `Reorder.Item`'s. The item in hand follows the pointer through the Batch, its `x` and `y` set at each move with no spring, over its Override stack, and through Auto-scroll; no Wardic element and no `createAnimate` (Q26). Let go, it springs to its place at its release speed, which the Batch takes as its start speed, as #665's hand-back does. **Its keys, blurs and presses** come through the feeds over the members too (Q28): focus through `focusAmong`, the focused item's own keys from its focus to its blur, as a Batch's press holds them (#460), and presses through `pointerAmong`. No item listens at rest, and no `keyboardAmong` feed is added.
8. **The motion config is the group's** (Q22), as `createBatch`'s is: `initial`, `exit`, `transition`, `whileDrag`, `whileHover` and `whileTap`, each values or a function of the item and its index. `BatchItem` takes its `value`, `as` (an `li`, as `Reorder.Item`), `ref`, `style`, its element's attributes and children. The group animates `x` and `y` to each place; a gesture's keys join them (Q23). Each item starts at its place, `initial` giving only the keys it names.
   - **`whileDrag`** is a layer over `whileTap` in each member's Override stack (ADR-0070 decision 12): on as an item is taken in hand, by a drag or a keyboard lift, off at its drop, back on the Batch's transition (Q27).
   - **One keyed Batch over `values`,** keyed by the value itself, each its own, as `Reorder.Group` needs. Its geometry is read where its places are: a change of it places every item again in one change, on the group's transition (Q30).
9. **In the first version** (Q4): `whileDrag` as Batch keys, with the item in hand raised by `z-index` on its element; `whileHover` and `whileTap`; items coming and going under `<AnimatePresence>` with `exit`; the server's start (#707). Out: variants, a layout projection, and anything else only a Wardic element has.
   - **`exit`** (Q38): an item leaving under `<AnimatePresence>` around the `<For>` plays it at its last place, its element kept until it lands and `inert` meanwhile; the others close up at once, and the group shrinks at once.
   - **The server's start** (Q39): each item at its place, `initial`'s keys over it, the group sized, `direction`, each item's tab stop; `aria-describedby` waits for the client, as `Reorder.Item`'s does. A grid with no `columns` (Q37) is laid out by CSS grid (`repeat(auto-fill, <width>px)` and the gaps), each item in flow, until the group reads its width, on the server and on the client alike, so hydrating keeps the markup.
   - **No gesture events on the group** (Q33): a `BatchItem` is a plain element, whose own DOM events work; a function `whileHover`, `whileTap` or `whileDrag` gives its keys from the first item, as `animate`'s do (Q34).
10. **A member's keys** (Q8): with the keyboard reorder on, Space and Enter lift and drop it, as on `Reorder.Item`, and don't press it; a pointer's press still runs `whileTap` and the tap events. With `keyboard={false}`, the keys press as on any Batch member. `keyboard` is read at each key's down (Q32, core's `PressOptions.keys` as a function), and a pressable group's items stay tab stops with the keyboard reorder off.
11. **Gated** (Q20): a `bench:solid` page pair, the Batch group against `Reorder.Group` at 1,000 and 5,000 items: mount at most 0.3× and a frame while dragging at most 0.75× of `Reorder.Group`'s. *Amended by* *Amendment (#714)*: rows of text of different heights, gated too.
12. **Shipped in 1.0.0** (Q1, Q9), in four stacked PRs (Q21): core's drag among members and the controller without a projection system; the components, with drag, keyboard, DOM order and `whileDrag`; hover and press, `exit`, the server's start, right-to-left and columns from the width; the docs, a sample and the bench pages.

## Considered Options

- **An option on `Reorder.Group`** (`batch={…}`), its `Reorder.Item`s becoming members (Q7 A). One prop to switch, but `Reorder.Item`'s props stay a Wardic element's, so a prop a member can't take is caught only at runtime.
- **A `reorder` option on `createBatch`'s keyed form** (Q7 B). It sits with the Batch, but the keyboard, announcements and Reorder's other props would get a second home.
- **A separate, smaller implementation**, as the prototype is (Q5 B): its own crossover, keyboard, live region and Auto-scroll. Faster to write, a second copy of all of it, and the overrides, right-to-left and reduced motion left out.
- **The nearest place as the crossover** (Q6), the prototype's: it agreed with Reorder's on the prototype's drags, and may differ on a diagonal one; one crossover for both forms won.
- **The app's own `place(i)`** (Q2 B): finding the place under the pointer would search them, or need the app's inverse too. **Items measured once** (Q2 C) is #714 (*Amendment (#714)*).

## Consequences

- **ADR-0020's "the items are ordinary `layout` + `drag` elements"** holds for `Reorder.Group`; a `BatchGroup`'s are Batch members (ADR-0020, *Amendment (#712)*).
- **ADR-0070 decision 12's "still none: drag, focus"** holds for a plain Batch; a `BatchGroup`'s members are dragged and focused through the group (ADR-0070, *Amendment (#712)*).
- **The DOM controller gains a second wiring:** its items' handles are structural (a projection handle is one), and what tells it the boxes are fresh is a Measure pass or the Batch's placing.
- **Uniform places are a limit:** a list of items of different sizes keeps `Reorder.Group` until #714. *Amended by* *Amendment (#714)*: it's a Batch Reorder group too.

## Note (#706, 2026-11-12): `initial`'s keys move

Decision 8's "each item starts at its place, `initial` giving only the keys it names" left those keys where `initial` put them: the group's only targets were its places, so `initial={{ opacity: 0 }}` held every item invisible. An item's `initial` keys but `x` and `y` now move to their identity as it enters (Q20), so `initial` is its entrance; with `appear`, that entrance plays from a server-rendered page's first paint (ADR-0044, *Amendment (#706)*).


## Amendment (#714, 2026-11-13): items of different sizes, the app's or read from the page

Decision 3 placed every item from one `itemSize`, so a list whose items differ in size kept `Reorder.Group` (*Uniform places are a limit*): rows as tall as their text, a kanban column's cards, a clip track. Grilled 2026-11-13 (#714, Q1–Q14), then prototyped.

**The prototype** (2026-11-13, `probe/714-sizes`, not merged; headless Chromium 147): rows of wrapping text, 240 px wide and one to three lines tall, in a column with a 4 px gap, against a `Reorder.Group` of the same rows in flow. Each figure is the median ratio to `Reorder.Group`'s, paired per rep, at 1,000 / 5,000 rows:

| ratio to `Reorder.Group` | mount | a frame while dragging | a frame at rest | the group's width changed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one size, 40 px (`itemSize={{ … }}`) | 0.24 / 0.28 | 0.50 / 0.32 | 0.84 / 0.49 | 0.01 / 0.00 |
| the app's (`itemSize={(item, i) => …}`) | 0.24 / 0.29 | 0.51 / 0.33 | 0.83 / 0.51 | 0.16 / 0.17 |
| read from the page (no `itemSize`) | 0.38 / 0.43 | 0.47 / 0.29 | 1.34 / 1.10 | 0.28 / 0.29 |

At 5,000 rows, `Reorder.Group` mounted in 387 ms, the read sizes in 167 ms and one size in 109 ms. A frame at rest is one the page draws while the group holds still, as it does while anything else on it animates: at 5,000 rows, 0.59 ms for `Reorder.Group`, 0.28 ms for one size and 0.68 ms for the read sizes. Every round checked that the rows were stacked a gap apart, that the group was as tall as its items after the mount and after the width change, and that the item dragged landed five places on; no ResizeObserver loop error was raised.

**Found by the prototype:**
- **A size changed inside the items' observer delivery raises the browser's loop error** ("ResizeObserver loop completed with undelivered notifications"). A frame's next delivery takes only elements deeper than the shallowest one just delivered, so the group's own size change, seen by an observer on the group or on anything above it (the page's, the projection's), is skipped. Writing the group's height in the delivery raised it once at a mount and twice at a width change. So the items move in the delivery, by their transforms alone, and the group's height waits (decision 5).
- **Flow before the first delivery is needed on the client too,** not only for the server (decision 4): items placed absolutely before their first size leave the group to change its height in the delivery, or to paint a frame at the wrong height. The switch from flow lays every row out a second time, about 10 ms at 1,000 rows and 50 ms at 5,000: the gap between the read sizes' mount and the 0.27 / 0.30 an absolute start measured.
- **The observer costs about 0.1 ms per 1,000 rows in every frame the page draws,** which puts a frame at rest above `Reorder.Group`'s. An idle page draws no frames, so it pays nothing.

**Decisions:**
1. **`itemSize` takes three forms** on `Reorder.BatchGroup`, on both bindings (Q1):
   - `{ width, height }`: every item one size, as decision 3's.
   - `(item, i) => ({ width, height })`: the app's size for each item. Nothing is read from the page, and the server places every item. It's tracked (Q8): a signal or store field it reads places every item again in one change on the group's transition, as a change of `gap` does (Q30). It's called for every item at each change of the list or the geometry.
   - Left out: each item's size is read from the page (decisions 3 and 4), its place from the sizes before it in the list's order, as `columns` left out is read from the width.
   - **A column's `width` can be left out** of a given size, an object or a function's (Q10): the item takes the group's content width, as a read item does (decision 2), and the group sets no width of its own, so a list of rows fills a responsive container. On `"x"` and `"xy"`, both are required.
   - **`columns` with a function or a left-out `itemSize` is a type error** (Q9): rows break at the group's width.
   - Rejected: B, read only, where an app that knows its sizes pays a read at mount and the server places nothing; C, the function only, with no rows as tall as their text, #714's first example. And, for `columns`, a row broken every `columns` items, as a CSS grid with auto rows: its flow on the server matches only when the widths are equal, and sizing the group's width gives the columns anyway.
2. **Shapes** (Q2, Q6):
   - `"y"`: a column, each item as wide as the group's content box and as tall as its own. The width is the group's content box's, its padding and border inside each item (`box-sizing: border-box`), exact on the server too: in flow, `100%`, the content box itself; placed, once the client has read the group's horizontal padding, `calc(100% - <that padding>)`, an absolutely placed item's `100%` being the group's padding box; placed before that read (a server's group of one size, or a function's), `100cqi`, the group a size container until the read. Grilled, it was read as a grid's is (decision 3) and written on the group as a CSS variable, whose fallback before the read, `100%`, had a padded group's server render overhang its padding until hydrated; changed in PR 1, the user's call, to container units throughout. Changed again in PR 3, the user's call, measured by its bench: under container units every change of the group's width restyled every item, and a group of rows read from the page took 0.50× and 0.51× `Reorder.Group`'s time to widen at 1,000 and 5,000 rows, over decision 6's 0.5, against 0.29× with `100%` in an A/B on the same pages; a frame at rest cost 1.48× at 5,000 rows, against 1.35× in the A/B (1.32× in PR 3's run of the change).
   - `"x"`: a row, each item its own width and height, at the row's top; the group is as tall as its tallest.
   - `"xy"`: rows, as CSS `flex-wrap` with `align-items: flex-start`: items left to right while they fit the group's content width, the next row below the tallest of the one before, each item its own size, at its row's top. Core's crossover is unchanged: a row is one line of overlapping y ranges (`Reorder.ts`'s lines).
   - Nothing is stretched: a stretched item would read the tallest one's height as its own.
   - Rejected, for a column's width: read and written as a CSS variable (Q6 as grilled, above).
   - Rejected: masonry, whose staggered columns overlap in y and so merge core's lines into one. No CSS layout every browser has lays it out on the server, and a move reshuffles every item after it across columns. And C, mixed sizes on `"y"` and `"x"` only.
3. **Sizes are read by one `ResizeObserver` per group, over every item, always on** (Q4). They arrive in its delivery, after layout and before paint: at mount, for an item added later, and when one changes (an image loads, a row expands, the group's width rewraps a row's text). An item's first size places it at once, with no flight from a guess, as a grid's first width does. A later change moves the items after it in one change on the group's transition; the item in hand keeps following the pointer while the others make room. It reads each item's border box (`borderBoxSize`), its layout size, so the Batch's own transforms (a `whileDrag` scale) never change one. The projection system observes its elements the same way (ADR-0018).
   - Rejected: B, sizes read at mount and at a width change only, the app reporting any other change itself (`group.measure(value)`): an image loading in a row leaves its place wrong until the app calls it.
4. **In flow until the first delivery** (Q5): on the server, and on the client until the observer's first delivery, the items are laid out by CSS (a flex column, a flex row, or `flex-wrap` with `align-items: flex-start`, the group's `gap` as CSS `gap`), each at no place. At the first delivery each item goes absolute at its place, where flow had it, so nothing moves and hydrating keeps the markup: decision 9's grid with no `columns`, extended. The function form is in flow only on `"xy"`, until the group's width is read. An item's margins count in flow but not in places, so the docs say to use `gap`.
   - Rejected: B, in flow for good, the Batch's `x` and `y` an offset from each item's flow position: every reorder lays the list out again, and shifts every moved member's values. C, absolute from the start: a pile at the group's corner until the first read, then the loop error or a frame at the wrong height.
5. **The group's height is written at the Frame loop's next tick** (found by the prototype): in the delivery the items move, and the group's new height waits for core's `Machine.nextFrame`, a one-shot that succeeds at the Frame loop's next tick (`@internal`, for the bindings). Grilled beside `LiveValue.eachFrame`, it moved to `Machine` in PR 2, the user's call: every app that animates has `Machine`, where importing `LiveValue` cost a Batch Reorder app 3.6 KB gzipped of its Atoms and scroll sources. At the switch from flow, flow's height is already the group's, so it's written with the switch.
   - Rejected: a `requestAnimationFrame` of the binding's own, outside the Frame loop.
6. **Gated** (Q7; G1–G3, accepted 2026-11-13): decision 11's page pair gains rows of text, the Batch group's three forms against `Reorder.Group` at 1,000 and 5,000 rows:
   - read from the page: a mount at most 0.5× `Reorder.Group`'s, a frame while dragging at most 0.75×, the group's width changed at most 0.5×, and a frame at rest at most 1.5×;
   - the app's: a mount at most 1.1× the one-size group's (1.03× at both sizes), and a frame while dragging at most 0.75× `Reorder.Group`'s.
   - Decision 11's gates hold for one size.
7. **The glossary's Batch Reorder group is widened, not renamed** (Q3): its places come from each item's own size and the sizes before it, sizes that are the app's, one or each, or read from the items. Reading an item's size isn't a Measurement, the projection's `Bounds`, so neither "re-measure" nor "a measured group" is a term.
8. **The docs** (Q13): the `reorder-batch` sample's switch gains "Rows of text", a column of cards read from the page; the Reorder guide shows the function form as a clip track (`axis="x"`, `itemSize={(clip) => ({ width: clip.seconds * 40, height: 56 })}`), and its "What it gives up" drops "every item is the one size".
   - Rejected: a second sample, more to keep up where the switch already compares shapes.
9. **Shipped in 1.1** (Q12), in three stacked PRs: given sizes (decisions 1 and 2, places on the server), with this Amendment and the glossary as its first commit and a minor changeset for `@wardic/solid`; sizes read from the page (decisions 3 to 5); the docs, the sample, and the bench pages with their gates (decisions 6 and 8). What a Batch Reorder app grows by is measured in each PR, the user's call at its merge.

**Consequences:**
- **Decision 3's "Every item is one size"** holds for the object form, and decision 4's items are sized from their own sizes.
- **Decision 11's gates** hold for one size; decision 6 gates the other two.
- ***Uniform places are a limit*** no longer holds: a list of items of different sizes is a Batch Reorder group, and reading its sizes costs an observer in every frame the page draws.
- **Core gains `Machine.nextFrame`,** `@internal`, a binding's way to wait for the Frame loop's next tick.

**Built, given sizes (PR 1;** `@wardic/solid`'s `batch-reorder-places.ts` and `batch-reorder.ts`, both bindings' `batch-reorder-group.tsx`**):**
- **Places:** `resolvePlaces` takes the list: one size as before, a column's width the group's content width when its size leaves it out; or each item's size, the function called once an item a change, laid out by axis (`layoutBy`): a column's running heights, a row's running widths, and rows that break where the next item passes the group's content width by more than a hundredth of a px (`FIT_SLACK`, for sums that round), each item at its row's top. Each item's place and size are `Float64Array`s (`ItemPlaces`), read by `placeOf`, `widthAt` and `heightAt`; `samePlaces` compares every item's place.
- **Its boxes:** every item's box for the Reorder group runs from the static position of an item with no width, the content box's start: right to left, each item's own static position is its right edge at the group's, so its box runs leftward from its place, by its own width (with one size, a shift every box shared). A reveal's box on the page is the item's element's at its static position, moved to its place.
- **The width a grid's rows break at** is the group's used width, unrounded, less its padding and border when it's border-box: `clientWidth`'s whole pixels broke rows where `flex-wrap` hadn't, a fraction of a pixel short. A group that scrolls on its own reads its client width, which leaves out its scrollbar. An item in flow doesn't shrink (`flex-shrink: 0`), so a row breaks in flow where its places do.
- **Styles:** the group sets no width of its own where its width is the app's (its rows break at it, or its items take it). Each item that takes it is `box-sizing: border-box` and as wide as decision 2 says (`groupWidthOf`), the group a size container only while it's `100cqi`; its box's width for the Reorder group is 0, a column's crossover reading its places' `y` alone. A grid of items each their own size, its width not read yet, is `display: flex`, `flex-wrap: wrap`, `align-items: flex-start`, its gap as `column-gap` and `row-gap`, each item in flow (decision 4's function form).
- **Tracked:** the function is called in the Batch's `each`, in the binding's tracking, and again for the group's style, which a change may run before or after the Batch's `each`: n more calls a change. Each item's size is written on its element at its `ref` when the places have its index (an item given sizes by a function only: a one-size item's `ref` is the Batch's), and at each change where it changed (`writeSizeOn`, the last write kept by element). Switched from one size to a function, every item's is written a microtask after the change: the group's style for its items drops the one size in a render effect after the change's. Switched back to one size with a width, an item that took the group's width gets its own `box-sizing` back (to one size with none, the group's style keeps it border-box: Solid 2 runs the change's apply after its style effects).
- **The server:** each item's own size in its style, its index from a map made at the first item that needs it. The size is laid over the item's own style, as the client writes it on its element: an own `width` or `height` gives way to the function's, which places it. (With one size, as before, an item's own style wins.)
- **Types:** `ReorderBatchSizing<V>`, `axis`, `itemSize` and `columns` together: a full size on any axis, a column's size with its `width` optional, each as an object (with `columns`) or a function (without). Split on `axis` alone, an `axis` typed as a union, as a sample's switch is, matched no member. `ReorderBatchColumnSize` is a column's size. `Reorder.BatchItem`'s props leave out its element's own `value` attribute: on Solid 1 an object value, a clip, was typed `V & (string | number)` and failed.
- **Defaults stated, not asked:**
  - an item that takes the group's width is border-box, as an item in flow fills its container with its border box; an item given a width keeps the app's `box-sizing`;
  - a group whose items take its width is a size container until the client reads its padding (a server's group of one size, or a function's), with its containment meanwhile: its own stacking context, the containing block of any fixed-position element inside it (an item's transform already is), and what a container query inside its items measures, so an item's own container query may change as it hydrates. An app's own `container-type` on it is kept over the group's;
  - its padding is read again the frame after its content box or its border box changes size (one observer each: a padding that changes moves one or the other); in that frame, the items are as wide as the padding before.
- **Pinned by** `reorder-batch.ssr.test.tsx` BS5–BS10 and BT1, on each binding: a column's, a row's, right to left, a column of the group's width, a grid in flow, `initial`; and the types (`columns` with a function, a size with no `width` off a column, an item's type from `values`, an object value). `reorder-batch.browser.test.tsx` BD1–BD6 (Solid 2's BD1–BD3, BD5, BD6), against a `Reorder.Group` of items of the same sizes laid out by the browser's flexbox: every place and the group's size, the same drags giving the same orders, a size changed in the function, a new order, a column of the group's width widened, an item added, a grid narrowed a fraction of a pixel, a switch between the forms (`box-sizing` with it), and a right-to-left row of different widths (BD7). **Built, sizes read from the page (PR 2;** `@wardic/core`'s `Machine.ts`, `@wardic/solid`'s `batch-reorder-places.ts` and `batch-reorder.ts`, both bindings' `batch-reorder-group.tsx`**):**
- **The observer:** one `ResizeObserver` per group, made at its first item, observing each item from its `ref` to its end (or its exit's start, under `<AnimatePresence>`), its size forgotten with it. It reads each item's border box (`borderBoxSize`), by value. A delivery with no new size does nothing, and an item with no size at all, under a hidden ancestor (a tab, a closed `<details>`), keeps its last: shown again, it's where it was, and hidden from the start, its group stays in flow.
- **Places:** `resolvePlaces` takes what was read (`SizesRead`): each item's size as read (a column's height alone, its width the group's, whether it has items or not), laid out as a function's sizes are; an item not read yet has no size, so it's placed after the items before it and moves the ones after it once it's read. Until the first sizes come in, and a grid's width, the places say `inFlow`, every item at no place.
- **Its binding's signals** (`BatchReorderSizeSignals`, each binding's own): `version`, at each delivery, read by the Batch's places; `placed`, at the first, read by the items' style and the group's; `groupVersion`, read by the group's style alone. The first delivery sets all three, in the delivery: the items go out of flow at once (`applyShifted`, as decision 9's grid read from its width), and the group's size goes with them, where flow had it already. A later one sets `version`, moving the items by their transforms on the group's transition, and the group's size waits for `Machine.nextFrame`, once for a frame's deliveries (decision 5). Solid 2 flushes each in the delivery, as its `onRead` does.
- **In flow** (decision 4): a column is a flex column, its gap `row-gap`, each item `width: 100%`, border-box, its content box's, and out of flow `calc(100% - <its padding>)`, the padding read before the first delivery, so its width doesn't change as it goes out of flow (decision 2); a row is a flex row at its items' tops, `width: max-content`, the width its places give it; a grid is `flex-wrap` rows, as a function's. An item in flow doesn't shrink (`flex-shrink: 0`).
- **Sizing switched at runtime:** to sizes read from the page, every listed item's element is observed, its items go into flow or to the places read so far at once, and a size a function wrote on it goes, a microtask after the change's style writes, where it's still the one written; from them, the observer is let go, and the sizes read stay for a switch back.
- **A `border-box` group** (found in review): the group's `height`, and a row's `width`, hold its padding and border when it's `border-box`, as a CSS reset makes it, read as it mounts and as it's resized (`BatchReorderRead`'s `edges`). Before, they were its content box's: a padded group under a reset was too small, whatever its sizing, since #712, and a group whose sizes are read changed size as it went out of flow, in its items' delivery. A server's knows no edges: one of one size, or a function's, comes out short of its padding there under a reset until it reads them.
- **Solid 1's first restyle** (found building BM12): the group writes its items' shared style a key at a time, each change removing the keys it drops, through `on(record, …, { defer: true })`; Solid's `on` hands a deferred effect's first change no previous input, so a first change left every key it dropped on the items since #712's Q47 (a switch from one size kept its `height`). The group keeps the style its items have now itself.
- **Types:** `ReorderBatchSizing<V>` gains a member with no `itemSize`, on any axis, without `columns` (Q9).
- **Core:** `Machine.nextFrame` (`@internal`), a machine of one Frame settled at its first step, run on the Frame loop: it succeeds at the next tick, keeping the loop ticking for that tick alone.
- **Defaults stated, not asked:** a grid's item wider than the group stays its max-content width in flow (`flex-shrink: 0`) and is as wide as the group once out of flow, an absolutely placed box's shrink-to-fit: its text wraps again as it goes out of flow, a size change in its own delivery, which raises the loop error once and places it the frame after.
